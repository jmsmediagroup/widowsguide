The money

Social Security widow’s benefits

When you can start, how much it can be, how long it lasts, and how to apply.

When you can start

  • At 60.
  • At 50, if you have a disability.
  • At any age, while you care for his child who is under 16, or disabled, and receives Social Security benefits.

Starting before your full retirement age for survivor benefits means a smaller monthly payment.

You generally must have been married to him for at least nine months before he died. There are exceptions, including an accidental death, a death on active military duty, and having a child together.

How much it can be

As a share of his benefit:

  • 71.5% if you start at 60, rising to 100% at your full retirement age for survivor benefits.
  • 71.5% for a widow with a disability who starts between 50 and 59.
  • 75% at any age while you care for his child.

If he claimed his own benefit early, yours can come to less than his full amount. It is then limited to the larger of what he was getting or 82.5% of his full benefit.

Your full retirement age for survivor benefits can come a few months before the one for your own retirement benefit. Social Security can tell you the dollar amount at each age you could start.

Your own benefit and his

If you also have a retirement benefit of your own, the two aren’t added together. You get the higher amount.

You may be able to take one now and switch to the other later. Ask which order works for you, with the figures for each.

How long it lasts

A widow’s benefit has no end date. Under Social Security’s rules it ends when you die, or when you start your own retirement benefit and it is as large as his full benefit or larger. A benefit based on a disability can end if the disability ends.

Remarrying before 60, or before 50 with a disability, usually stops it, and it can restart if that later marriage ends. Remarrying at 60 or later doesn’t stop it. At 62 you may be able to take a benefit on the new husband’s record, if it would be higher.

If your benefit is the one for caring for his child, ask Social Security what changes as the child grows older.

If you work

Below full retirement age, earnings above a yearly limit can reduce a survivor benefit. For 2026 the limit is $24,480, and $65,160 in the year you reach full retirement age. For 2027 the two limits are $25,560 and $67,920.

This test uses your full retirement age for your own benefit, which can be later than the one for survivor benefits. Money held back is generally made up once you reach full retirement age. From that month on, earnings don’t reduce the benefit.

If you have a government pension

The Social Security Fairness Act, signed January 5, 2025, ended the offset that used to cut a widow’s benefit by two-thirds of a government pension from work without Social Security taxes. It applies to benefits from January 2024. If you were turned down or paid less because of it, ask again.

How to apply

Survivor benefits can’t be applied for online. Call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 to book an appointment, or visit a local office. Call early.

When you apply can matter. Before your full retirement age for survivor benefits, a benefit usually isn’t paid for the months before you apply. After it, it can usually be paid up to six months back. There are exceptions, including some for an application made soon after his death, so ask about them when you call.

Reporting his death is a separate step: how to tell Social Security someone has died. So is the one-time $255 payment, with two years to apply: the Social Security death benefit.

What to ask

  1. What would my survivor benefit be at each age I could start?
  2. What would my own benefit be, and could I take one first and switch later?
  3. When is my full retirement age for survivor benefits, and for my own benefit?
  4. How far back can my benefit be paid if I apply now?
  5. If I work, how would my earnings be counted?

Ask for the figures in writing, and take them to someone with nothing to sell you before you choose. The chapter that owns this is What happens with Social Security? Benefits from a pension, OPM or the VA are in survivor benefits after a husband dies.
