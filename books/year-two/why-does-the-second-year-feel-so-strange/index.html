Book Seven · Year Two · Chapter 1

Why does the second year feel so strange?

“Everyone thinks I'm fine now. I'm not sad the way I was. I'm just… lost.”

Home › The books › Year Two › Chapter 1

The short answer

The second year can feel like this. The sharp grief can soften, many of the tasks may be done, and other people may think you are fine.

What is left can be quieter and harder to name. It is a life that no longer has its old shape, and does not yet have a new one.

Not every widow feels this, and the second year can also feel steadier than the first. If you do feel lost, it does not mean you have gone backward.

What to do

  1. Tell one person how the second year actually feels, in a sentence or two.
  2. Notice one hour a week that feels like yours, and write down what you did in it.
  3. Write down anything you are curious about, however small.
  4. Open anything dated, or from Social Security, a pension plan, a health plan, Medicare or an insurer, the day it arrives.
  5. Ask whoever is handling the estate or your taxes whether anything is still open.
  6. Read What do I actually want? when you feel ready to try something.

What can wait

    Who can help

    A grief group that includes widows further along can help in the second year, and so can a counselor. Book 5, The First Year, covers both in Would a grief group or counselor help?

    If you have thoughts of not wanting to be here, tell someone today. You can call or text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org. It is for anyone who is struggling, and you do not have to be suicidal to reach out.

    For most people who have these thoughts, support and treatment work. If the thoughts feel like a plan, call or text 988 now.

    You can also text HOME to 741741 to reach the Crisis Text Line. The Veterans Crisis Line is 988, then press 1, or text 838255, and it also serves families and friends. Where to get help tonight, at the back of this book, lists every line.

    Checked October 2026 against 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, Social Security Administration, National Institute on Aging. Rules and numbers change, so check anything you rely on with the office itself.
